## Closest prior work

| # | Paper | What it does | Relation to this project |
|---|-------|--------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Ho, Jain, Abbeel. *Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models*, NeurIPS 2020 (arXiv:2006.11239) | Introduces the `L_simple` ε-prediction loss with uniform t and i.i.d. ε. | Our baseline estimator, exactly. |
| 2 | Nichol & Dhariwal. *Improved DDPM*, ICML 2021 (arXiv:2102.09672) | Importance-samples t with p(t) ∝ sqrt(E[L_t²]) (10-sample history per t) to reduce variance of the **L_vlb** objective. Reports that it helps L_vlb but not the hybrid objective. | Our importance-sampling arm reuses their proposal rule, but applied to `L_simple` with unbiased reweighting, and measured on **gradient** variance rather than loss variance. |
| 3 | Kingma, Salimans, Poole, Ho. *Variational Diffusion Models*, NeurIPS 2021 (arXiv:2107.00630) | Uses a "low-discrepancy" timestep sampler (t_i = (u + i/k) mod 1 — a randomly shifted lattice / systematic stratification) and shows it reduces VLB-estimator variance; also learns the schedule to minimise variance. | Stratified/QMC timesteps are **not new**; our stratified and Sobol arms are re-tests of this idea on `L_simple` DDPM at equal compute. |
| 4 | Song, Durkan, Murray, Ermon. *Maximum Likelihood Training of Score-Based Diffusion Models*, NeurIPS 2021 (arXiv:2101.09258) | Importance sampling of continuous time for the likelihood-weighted loss; large loss-variance reductions. | Prior evidence that IS over t is effective when the per-t weighting is very non-uniform; our `L_simple` setting is much flatter. |
| 5 | Kingma & Gao. *Understanding Diffusion Objectives as the ELBO with Simple Data Augmentation*, NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2303.00848) | Shows that the timestep/noise-level sampling density and the loss weighting are interchangeable up to variance. | Theoretical justification for our framing: IS with unbiased reweighting keeps the objective fixed and only changes variance. |
| 6 | Xu, Tong, Jaakkola. *Stable Target Field for Reduced Variance Score Estimation*, ICLR 2023 (arXiv:2302.00670) | Reduces variance of the DSM *regression target* by using a reference batch (a self-normalised importance-sampling estimate of the true score). | 7 | Hang et al. *Efficient Diffusion Training via Min-SNR Weighting*, ICCV 2023 (arXiv:2303.09556) | Re-weights per-t losses (multi-task view) for ~3x faster convergence. | Changes the *objective*. We deliberately keep the objective fixed so that any gain is attributable to variance alone. |
| 8 | Wang et al. *SpeeD: A Closer Look at Time Steps is Worthy of Triple Speed-Up*, CVPR 2025 (arXiv:2405.17403) | Asymmetric timestep sampling + change-aware weighting. | Biased w.r.t. L_simple (changes the effective objective); out of scope. |
| 9 | Kim, Ki, Shim, Lee. *Adaptive Non-uniform Timestep Sampling for Accelerating Diffusion Model Training*, CVPR 2025 (arXiv:2411.09998)† | Observes gradient variance varies strongly with t; adaptively up-samples timesteps whose updates matter most. | Closest in motivation (gradient variance per t). Their sampler is not an unbiased IS estimator of a fixed loss. We measure the per-t gradient variance directly and test a classical unbiased IS. |
| 10 | Sun & Shi. *Variance-Aware Adaptive Weighting for Diffusion Model Training*, 2026 (arXiv:2603.10391)† | Analyses loss variance across log-SNR and proposes adaptive log-SNR reweighting that approximates variance-aware IS (EDM, CIFAR-10). | Recent and close; they modify the weighting, we compare unbiased classical estimators at a fixed weighting. |
| 11 | *CARV: Variance Reduction for Expectations with Diffusion Teachers*, ICML 2026 (arXiv:2605.21489)† | Importance sampling + stratification (one sample per bin through the IS inverse CDF) of timesteps, for expectations under **frozen** diffusion teachers (distillation, attribution). Reports ~1.2x variance reduction from the proposal. | Same estimator family as our stratified/IS arms, but for frozen teachers, not from-scratch training. |
| 12 | Jeha et al. *Variance Reduction of Diffusion Model's Gradients with Taylor Approximation-Based Control Variate*, ICML 2024 workshop (arXiv:2408.12270)† | Control variate from a Taylor expansion of the training objective. Clear gains on a toy problem; *inconclusive on MNIST*, suggests Adam may mask variance. | The closest "finance-style variance reduction for diffusion training" work. Their null result on MNIST is a prior we take seriously. We do not implement a control variate (time budget). |
| 13 | *Control Variate Score Matching for Diffusion Models*, 2025 (arXiv:2512.20003)† | Control variate combining denoising and target score identities; needs a target energy (sampler learning). | Not applicable to data-driven image training (no energy). |
| 14 | *Antithetic Noise in Diffusion Models*, ICLR 2026 (arXiv:2506.06185)† | Pairs each **initial sampling noise** with its negation at inference time; strongly negatively correlated samples, narrower CIs. ## How this project differs, and what we can honestly claim

* **Not novel:** importance sampling of timesteps (2, 4, 9, 10, 11) and stratified / low-discrepancy timesteps (3, 11). These arms are *replications at equal compute* on the plain `L_simple` DDPM objective.
* **Not found in prior work (to the extent of this search):** antithetic ε-pairs inside the *training* loss estimator of a DDPM. Antithetic noise has been used at inference (14) and antithetic couplings for other gradient estimators (15). We claim only that we could not find it, not that it does not exist.
* **The contribution we aim for is a controlled comparison and an explanation**: a single codebase where only the (x0, t, ε) estimator changes, equal (x0, t, ε) evaluations per step, directly measured gradient variance at identical parameters, and a law-of-total-variance decomposition (x0 / t / ε components, antithetic correlation ρ per t) that predicts which technique can help. If none of the techniques change FID, that is reported as the result (consistent with the inconclusive MNIST finding of 12).
